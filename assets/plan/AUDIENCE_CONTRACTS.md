# Five audience purposes

All five depths are required for every in-scope subject. Each needs a distinct explanation and acceptance evidence; an overview does not substitute for a working explanation, and a detailed map does not substitute for the earlier teaching views. They can share a file when each is identifiable and directly accessible.

Depth is a combination of prior knowledge, purpose and precision. A senior leader may need D4; a specialist entering a new field may need D1. Use reader needs rather than job titles to select content.

| Depth | Reader question | Essential explanation | Suitable visual forms | Acceptance question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 Everyday meaning | What is this, why does it matter, and what changes? | Concrete situation, recognizable need or phenomenon, key transformation or relationship, one limit | Before and after, annotated scene, simple comparison, literal worked example | Explain the idea to a newcomer and name one thing it does not imply |
| D2 Orientation and judgment | How does the whole fit together, and what choices or implications matter? | Major capabilities or themes, dependencies, stakeholders where applicable, material tradeoffs and uncertainty | Overview, comparison matrix, system boundary, annotated thematic map | Explain the whole, identify a dependency or competing interpretation, and ask an informed question |
| D3 Working understanding | How can I follow, use or reason through it? | Necessary concepts in context, mechanisms or analytical steps, meaningful transitions, ordinary interruption or counterexample | Worked sequence, actor lanes, concept relationship, timeline with evidence, annotated derivation | Trace an ordinary case, explain why a step matters and reason about a variation |
| D4 Domain expertise | Which precise distinctions explain behavior or support the interpretation? | Selected contracts, methods, assumptions, models, evidence disagreements and failure or boundary cases | State view, technical architecture, model comparison, argument map, proof structure | Predict a new case or defend an interpretation with its assumptions and limits |
| D5 Detailed map and precise evidence | How are the in-scope details connected, and where can I verify them? | Detailed entities or concepts, labeled relationships, boundaries, provenance and investigation paths | Mandatory detailed visualization or map, supported by sources and references | Trace a meaningful relationship across the map, locate its supporting evidence and explain a limit |

## D1 Everyday meaning

Use plain, respectful language. A concrete story can explain a phenomenon without inventing a beneficiary or a product benefit. Define necessary terms where used. Keep essential meaning visible; a glossary alone does not explain the story.

An analogy must map its important parts and state where it breaks. Prefer the literal example if the analogy adds more concepts than it removes.

## D2 Orientation and judgment

Do not force ROI, management roles or strategic decisions onto a natural phenomenon, historical question or mathematical idea. Explain significance, competing accounts or important relationships as appropriate. Separate promised capability from observed result. Do not invent measurements.

## D3 Working understanding

Show what changes, what is carried forward and why. For a process, identify responsibilities and handoffs; for a topic, show concepts, reasoning and examples. Do not invent workflow actors where none exist.

## D4 Domain expertise

Technical depth is domain-specific: interfaces for software, assumptions and methods for science, historiography for history, derivations for mathematics, decision rules for a procedure. Select the distinctions that explain the subject rather than listing everything.

A changed case can be an interaction, a paired static diagram, a worked counterexample or an exercise with an explanation. Interactivity is useful only when the reader learns from the consequence.

## D5 Detailed map and precise evidence

A detailed visualization or map is mandatory for each in-scope subject in every build, alongside mandatory D1-D4 explanations. It is the deepest inspection view. D4 curates the distinctions needed to reason about behavior; D5 exposes the detailed structure within the declared scope so readers can explore and verify those distinctions.

Map the actual subject: components and contracts for software; concepts, mechanisms and evidence for science; actors, events, relationships and competing interpretations for history; definitions, dependencies and proof relationships for mathematics. Choose appropriate semantics rather than forcing everything into a pipeline.

The map must provide:

- Explicit subject boundaries and a coverage statement.
- Identifiable entities, concepts, states, events or artifacts at the relevant level of precision.
- Labeled relationships that distinguish causation, chronology, dependency, association and inference as appropriate.
- A legend, meaningful grouping and a way to follow a representative path or relationship.
- Precise source references for material claims, with dates or versions where relevant.
- Visible uncertainty, disputed connections and evidence gaps.
- Legibility at the intended viewing size and an accessible equivalent explanation.

Static diagrams, linked panels and interactive maps are all valid when suited to the medium. A long document may need a full-resolution companion map. A source list, collection of disconnected cards or generic overview does not satisfy D5.

Reuse and inspect a suitable existing map; preserve any material designated immutable. If it is missing or insufficient, produce the required new map or supplementary map without editing protected originals. Do not invent relationships to make it look complete. Missing material evidence can leave a candidate incomplete, but cannot justify claiming that a source index satisfies this requirement.

## Across the depths

- Keep one coherent subject identity and anchor case, with a short recap for direct entrants.
- Make central concepts and limits visible without opening every disclosure.
- Label important relationships; distinguish causation, association, chronology, containment and inference.
- Keep historical evidence, current observation, proposed design and invented examples distinct where the claim appears.
- Use different views because they answer different questions, not because each repeats the same diagram with more boxes.
- Preserve meaningful reading order and accessibility in the chosen medium.
- Link the next depth through a question the reader now wants answered.
- Define teach-back questions that expose misconceptions, not only recognition or terminology recall.

## Evidence categories

Report separately: source fidelity, artifact functionality or rendering, expert instructional judgment, actual audience comprehension and measured presentation duration. Evidence in one category does not establish another.

Unless explicitly required by the application, an unperformed learner study is a reported limit rather than an endless implementation blocker. Never label expert simulated teach-back as a real participant result.
