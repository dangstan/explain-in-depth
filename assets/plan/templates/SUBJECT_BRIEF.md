# Subject design worksheet

G5-02 creates one completed subjects/<SUBJECT_ID>.md per bounded subject. This template is not source evidence.

## Subject and purpose

Record the subject, reader need, scope boundary, current knowledge assumptions and concrete learning outcomes. Name the pilot rationale if applicable.

## Source basis

List exact source locations, date/version, authority, competing accounts and unresolved gaps. Point to run/CLAIMS.md instead of duplicating long extracts.

## Coherent case

Choose one literal or explicitly illustrative example across depths. State what it demonstrates, what it cannot establish and which variation tests understanding. Use an analogy only with a mapping and a limit.

## Depth designs

For each mandatory depth D1, D2, D3, D4 and D5, record:
- Audience question and prior knowledge.
- Outcome expressed as explain, predict, distinguish, perform or locate.
- Essential concepts and locally defined terms.
- Main visual or explanatory structure and why it fits.
- Normal case and meaningful changed case, counterexample or alternative interpretation.
- Essential visible content versus optional inspection.
- Detail intentionally omitted.
- Source support and local uncertainty labels.
- Direct-entry recap and next-question transition.
- Discriminating teach-back and expected evidence.
- Exact destination in run/OUTPUTS.md.

No depth may be omitted. Specify distinct learning outcomes, destinations and acceptance evidence for all five. Reduce the breadth of the subject when necessary rather than dropping a depth; record material subject-scope changes before implementation.

For D5, specify the map's coverage boundary, entities, relationship semantics, visual grammar, legend, source references, uncertainty treatment, representative investigation path and exact map destination. Identify whether to preserve and reuse an existing map or create a new/supplementary one.

## Companions and integration

Include only requested companions. Name their audience, purpose, source depths and individual acceptance criteria. Keep the explanation coherent across formats without compressing every depth into every companion.
